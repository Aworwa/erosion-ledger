Method & Sources

The identity. With d = debt/GDP, r = average nominal interest rate on the stock, g = nominal GDP growth, and p = primary deficit (% of GDP): d′ = d × (1+r)/(1+g) + p. The change is approximately (r − g) × d + p. When g exceeds r, the existing stock shrinks relative to the economy; the primary deficit is the new borrowing piled on top. Interest is excluded from p because it already lives inside r — adding the headline deficit would double-count it.

Debt measure. Debt held by the public (Treasury “Debt to the Penny”), not gross debt — intragovernmental holdings are the government owing itself. End-of-quarter level over that quarter's nominal GDP (SAAR).

Nominal GDP. BEA quarterly releases, current-dollar GDP, SAAR percent change. 2026 Q2 = 8.0% (2nd est.), Q1 = 6.1%. Levels chained back from the Q2 2026 level of $32.49T; small differences vs. vintage levels reflect annual revisions.

Average rate. Treasury Fiscal Data, “Average Interest Rates on U.S. Treasury Securities,” total interest-bearing debt, quarter-end month. It moves slowly: the stock reprices only as old paper rolls, which is exactly why issuance strategy matters.

Primary deficit. Headline deficit less net interest, from Treasury Monthly Statements and CRFB tallies: FY24 ≈ $0.95T (3.3% of GDP), FY25 ≈ 2.7%, FY26 running ≈ 3.0–3.1% (10-month deficit $1.8T, gross interest $1.17T). Quarterly values are the fiscal-year rate, an estimate by construction.

Scenario engine. Annual steps from 2026 Q2. Coupon path: r′ = r + roll × (market − r). Nominal g = real + inflation. Bondholder real return = average coupon minus inflation — the erosion machine's fuel is exactly that spread when it's negative.
